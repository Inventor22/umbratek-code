Joint limits used by the jog panel come from arm_description/urdf/utra6_1000.urdf.xacro
(J3 +/-2.8811 rad; the others +/-(2*pi - 0.02) rad), capped at +/-180 deg for planned moves. The same file
rates the joint speeds at 2.0, 2.0, 2.1, 3.0, 3.0, 3.0 rad/s, faster than this arm's servos allow, so the panel
caps joint moves from the servos' own limits instead (see JOINT_RATED_DEG_S in scripts/jog_server.py).
The URDF's forward kinematics match the controller's reported TCP pose to 0.1 mm / 0.1 deg.

collision.json is ours, fitted to those meshes for the jog panel's collision checks (its capsules and base by
tools/model/fit_capsules.py):
  - Each moving link is covered by 1-5 capsules along its joint axes and tube. Every mesh vertex, plus
    surface samples about 0.7 mm apart, lies inside one; each capsule is the smallest-volume one around its
    line that covers its points. The tubes are split where their radius changes, so the upper arm's
    capsule is 52.5 mm against the tube's 52 mm, the forearm's 43.5 mm against 43 mm. Housings are
    looser: a capsule's round ends bulge past a motor housing's flat face by up to about 45 mm.
  - The base is the cylinder around its mesh (78.6 mm radius, 56 mm tall).
  - skip lists the link pairs that are within 10 mm of each other at every pose, by design: the base and
    upper arm (always about 3 mm apart), the forearm and wrist 2 (their capsules always overlap), wrist 1
    and the flange (always about 1 mm). Checked over 3000 random poses, as MoveIt's setup assistant does.
    Neighbouring links aren't checked against each other either.
The tool flange is modelled, but not anything mounted on it.
