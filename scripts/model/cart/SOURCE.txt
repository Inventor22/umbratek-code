The aluminium-extrusion cart the UTRA 6-1000 stands on, for the jog panel's 3D view and collision checks.

Source: Dustin's SolidWorks assembly "UTRA 1000 Aluminum Extrusion Assembly.SLDASM" in
https://github.com/Inventor22/umbratek-cad (folder "Utra 1000", last changed in commit 1aec164),
read with SolidWorks 2026 (3DEXPERIENCE R2026x) through its COM API, without saving anything back:
one STL per component in assembly coordinates, plus each component's placement.
Made by tools/model/sw_export2.py, sw_export3.py and build_cart2.py (tools/model/README.md).

Frame: everything is in the arm's URDF base_link frame (z up; origin at the centre of the base's
mounting face, where the base meets the mounting plate). The assembly's 1000-BASE part is lined up with
Umbratek's URDF base: its cylinder axis and mounting face come from the part's own geometry, and its turn
about that axis is the best fit of the two base meshes (1.2 mm mean gap; the next best, 180 degrees off,
is 1.7 mm). So the arm stands on the cart as it does in the CAD; if the real arm was mounted turned on its
plate, the cart will look turned in the 3D view relative to the real one.

utra_cart.urdf:
  - visual: meshes/frame.STL (extrusions, brackets, plate, plank, screws), meshes/casters.STL and
    meshes/controller.STL (the NTRO controller), lightly decimated by vertex clustering (1.5 mm for the
    frame, 4 mm for the casters and controller).
  - collision: one box per part along the part's own axes, tight around its mesh; the round mounting plate
    is a cylinder (85 mm radius, 12.7 mm thick). Collision names are what the jog panel's messages call
    each part. The floor is a thin box whose top touches the bottoms of the casters, 730 mm below the
    base's mounting face.
  - The arm's own parts in the assembly, and the hidden "5kg cube" weights, are left out.
