# Programs, teaching and the cell

Below the joints and the tool, the [jog panel](jog-panel.md) has three more sections for machine-tending work: **Program**, **Teach** and **Cell**. The server keeps everything in a folder (`cell/` in the repo, which git ignores; pick another with `--cell DIR`) and runs programs itself, so a run carries on with the page closed and is supervised by the server, not the page. `docs/decisions.md` records why it works the way it does.

- **The cell** (`cell/cell.json`): *tools*, *grippers*, *frames*, *targets*, *patterns*, *interlocks* and *keep-out zones*. Every change is saved at once with a new revision; a page holding an older revision is refused rather than overwriting someone else's change. A cell saved before grippers, interlocks and patterns existed reads as having none, unchanged.
  - A **tool** is its tool point (mm from the flange), its payload (kg, and centre of gravity in mm) and its shape: a capsule from the flange towards the tool point, which joins the collision check. **Use** makes it the tool in use and sends its payload to the controller. The controller's own tool offset stays zero: the panel applies the tool point itself (`docs/decisions.md`, D14). To find a tool point you can't measure, name the tool, rest its point on one fixed spike from four or more well-turned angles and press **Touch** at each, then **Fit**: the panel solves for the point and reports how well the touches agree (RMS).
  - A **gripper** is bound to outputs: the output and value that close it (the other value opens it; two or more outputs, a double-acting valve's, through the API), how long it takes, and optionally inputs that read on when it has closed, when it has opened, and when it has closed on a part. With an input to confirm, opening or closing waits for it (up to the gripper's timeout, then the step fails); without one, it waits the gripper's set time. A grasp then reads the "on a part" input, if there is one, and fails without a part. A grasp switches the controller's payload to the tool's and the part's together (`set_tcp_load`; the part's mass and centre of gravity, from the tool point, come with the step), and opening switches it back. In freedrive the payload waits until freedrive ends. The rows' **Open**, **Close** and **Grasp** do the same from the page (a grasp from the page doesn't know the part's mass). STOP doesn't change a gripper's outputs: what it holds stays held.
  - A **frame** is a fixture's position and orientation (in the base, or in another frame). Name it, touch its origin, a point along its +X edge and a point on its +Y side with the tool point, then **Fit**. Targets and zones are stored in their frame, so recalibrating a frame after moving the fixture moves every target in it: the message says how many moved, and flags any now out of reach or in another arm configuration.
  - A **target** is a tool-point pose in a frame (with the tool and the joint angles it was taught with, which keep the arm's elbow and wrist the way they were), or plain joint angles. **Plan** beside a target stages a joint move to it, just as dragging does: the shadow arm and the plan bar show it, and nothing moves until you press **Move**. Straight moves to targets run as program steps (**Step** runs one at a time). **Touch up** re-teaches a target in place, where the arm is now (a tool target keeps its frame and tool, which must be the tool in use; a joint target takes the joints): the message says how far it moved, along its frame's axes, and how far it turned, the row keeps saying so, and the 3D view rings where it was. Every program's move to it, and any pattern it's a corner of, changes with it. **Undo** puts the old value back. Every change to a target (taught, taught again, touched up, undone, deleted) goes to `cell/logs/targets.jsonl` with its old value and its new.
  - A **pattern** is a grid of slots, rows × columns (× layers), taught by three tool targets in one frame: its first slot, the last slot of its first row and the last slot of its first column (one of them is enough for one row or one column). The slots fill the grid between them, numbered along the first row, then the next; layers stack the given pitch apart along the frame's z (negative to stack downwards). Every slot takes the first slot's orientation, and is solved from the corners' taught joints, interpolated. Its *counter* is the next slot to use; a count step moves it on, and once every slot is used it's *used up*. Counters are kept in `cell/counters.json`, so they carry on after a restart, and changing one doesn't change the cell. **Reset** puts a counter back to slot 1, and **Plan** stages a joint move to the next slot.
  - A **keep-out zone** is a box in a frame: no part of the arm or tool may come within 10 mm of it. The 3D view draws zones as faint boxes, frames as a short light line along their x axis with their name, targets as dots, and patterns' slots as smaller grey dots.
  - An **interlock** is a named condition on one input (a machine's door open, its spindle stopped). A keep-out zone can be *guarded* by interlocks (**Open while**): the arm may enter it while they all hold, and it's a keep-out zone while any doesn't. Every move that would enter a guarded zone reads its interlocks just before it's planned, and is refused while one doesn't hold, saying which and what its input reads: program steps, moves to targets, the brain's paths and bursts, safe home, jogs and **Move** alike (a dry run assumes they'll hold, as it assumes inputs arrive, and says so). As the arm moves into the zone the server reads them again every 0.1 s: if one stops holding, it stops the arm, pauses the run (a move started from the page stops) and calls the owner; Resume reads them again. A move out of a zone needs no interlock. The brain is told only of the zones that are closed. The 3D view draws a guarded zone with dashed edges, and without its fill while it's open; the interlock's lamp is lit green while it holds. An interlock keeps the arm out of a machine that isn't ready for it; it isn't a safety function (it's read through the controller's ordinary inputs, a tenth of a second apart), so a machine's own guarding still protects people.
- **Programs** (`cell/programs/<name>.json`) are lists of steps: joint and straight moves to targets or to a pattern's next slot (optionally backed off along the tool, at their own speed), planned moves and learned skills (below), gripper steps (open, close, grasp), counting a pattern on (or back to slot 1), waits (for a time, or for an input with a timeout), setting or pulsing an output, Retry and Fallback (below), safe home, calling the owner, halts (pause with a message for the operator) and comments. Repeats, ifs and calls to other programs are written in the JSON (see `scripts/runner.py`) and shown in the list; the page edits the top level, repeats, retries and fallbacks. Click a step to select it: new steps and recorded targets go after it; select an empty retry's (or fallback's) row, or a fallback's **If it fails**, to add steps into it. A target, gripper or pattern used by a program can't be deleted.
- **Recovery.** A step that can't do its job *fails*: it can't be planned or reached, the brain finds no path, a wait or a gripper's input times out, a grasp finds no part, a pattern is used up, an interlock keeps a zone closed. A failure happens with the arm at rest, before anything is sent or once a move has arrived. A **Retry** step (up to n tries) runs its steps again from the first when one fails; a **Fallback** runs its steps, and if one fails, the steps under **If it fails** instead (they're numbered on from its steps). Either carries on only if the arm is still ready; a failure that nothing catches stops the arm and faults the run, as failures always have. A *fault* is never caught: the controller reporting an error or losing status, the arm leaving its path or not arriving, the brain's proposal refused. **Safe home** backs the tool straight out along its own axis (as far as it can go clear of everything, up to the step's distance, and says how far if that's short), then makes a joint move to a target, each move planned and checked as any is; a pause during it carries on with the rest. **Go home**, beside the run keys, runs it as a move to a target. **Call the owner** logs a message and shows it in the Program section's *Calls* until it's seen (a phone notification later); a run that faults calls the owner too, as does an interlock that stops a run. `GET /api/status` lists the calls not yet seen, so another program can pass them on.
- **The brain: planned moves and learned skills.** A *planned move* goes to a target along a path the brain plans afresh each run, around what's in the cell (its keep-out zones and the tool), ending in the configuration the target was taught in. A *learned skill* handles the variable part of a job: the brain's skill acts in short bursts, at most ten a second, keeping the tool point within a set distance of a target, until it says it's done; then an input can be checked (written in the JSON, `"check"`). The brain is a separate process on this PC, which the server asks (`--brain`, by default `http://127.0.0.1:8770`; it may run in WSL2). Until the real one exists, start the stub: `python brain/stub/stub_brain.py`. The brain only proposes: the server checks everything it proposes, whole, before anything moves (limits, collisions with the arm, cart, floor, tool and zones, speeds, and that the arm is still where the brain was told it was), and refuses it with its reasons otherwise, which faults the run. Those steps carry a grey cue naming the brain's part, or a red one when the brain isn't answering or lacks the skill, and a run that needs the brain won't start without it. STOP stops them as anything else, even while the brain is thinking. `docs/brain-interface.md` specifies the interface.
- **Running:** **Run** runs from the top, **Run from** on a step runs from there, **Step** runs one step and waits, **Dry run** plans every move and checks it for collisions without moving anything or setting outputs (a wait for an input assumes it arrives; an if reads the input as it is; a planned move asks the brain for its path; a learned skill is assumed to end where it began; a gripper is assumed to confirm and hold its part; patterns count on a copy of their counters; guarded zones' interlocks are assumed to hold) and says how long the run would take. **Pause** stops the arm; **Resume** re-plans the interrupted step from wherever the arm is, first moving back onto the straight line it was on, if it was on one. **Stop run** ends it. A step that can't be done (out of reach, blocked, the arm knocked off its path, an input that never came, a controller error) stops the arm and *faults* the run, unless it's a failure that a Retry or Fallback around it carries on from (Recovery, above); when a move would have collided, the 3D view shows where, as it does for a blocked plan. Fix the cause and press **Reset**. Every move is planned and collision-checked before it's sent, and watched against its plan while it runs. Programs run at their own speeds (by default 50 mm/s for lines, 20 °/s for joint moves) times the **Override** (10 to 100%); straight lines, and whatever the brain proposes, are capped at 250 mm/s at the tool unless the program's JSON says `"fullSpeed": true`. With the server's reduced speed on ([jog panel](jog-panel.md)), every move is slowed until neither the tool point nor the flange can pass 250 mm/s, joint moves included, whatever the program says; with its hold-to-move on, a run starts, resumes or steps only with the hold-to-move key held, and pauses if it's let go (Resume, with it held again, carries on; a move to a target stops instead). A dry run needs no hold. Jogging is refused while a program moves the arm and allowed while it's paused.
- **The log:** the Program section lists the run's events; every run is also written to `cell/logs/<date>/<time>-<program>.jsonl` (`~2`, `~3` added before `.jsonl` for another run of the same program started in the same second).

- **Teaching:** hold **Hold for freedrive** until the amber bar fills, guide the arm by hand, and press **Record here** at each pose: as a tool pose in the chosen frame or as joint angles, and with **Then add** set to a straight or joint move, a move to it is added to the program after the selected step. Freedrive starts only from READY. Press the key again to leave: the controller switches the motors off onto their brakes (the arm settles about half a millimetre; recorded targets are from before that), and the panel switches them back on with Enable's checks. STOP in freedrive holds the arm rigid, then switches the motors off; freedrive also ends on its own after 10 minutes, motors off. **Sensitivity** is the controller's teach sensitivity, 90, 100 or 110 % (set it before entering; writing it stops the arm). On this arm 90, the default, stops where you let go; 100 and 110 make the shoulder and elbow lighter, but a raised elbow sinks back a few degrees. Don't turn a joint by hand past about ±350 degrees: the controller faults at its limit (see [troubleshooting](troubleshooting.md)).

The arm's state arrives 10 times a second (`data/captures/2026-10-21/README.md`), and that rules some things out: a move can't stop on contact by watching the joints, for one, as a status frame shows a change up to a tenth of a second after it happens.

The server's API (JSON; every POST needs the page's token, so only the page can call it, or the program that started the server and chose the token: see [jog-panel.md](jog-panel.md)): `GET /api/status`, `/api/cell`, `/api/programs`, `/api/program?name=`, `/api/run`, `/api/io`, `/api/calls`; `POST /api/cell/tool`, `/api/cell/tool/activate`, `/api/cell/tool/delete`, `/api/cell/tool/touch`, `/api/cell/tool/touch/clear`, `/api/cell/tool/calibrate`, `/api/cell/gripper`, `/api/cell/gripper/delete`, `/api/cell/frame`, `/api/cell/frame/delete`, `/api/cell/frame/touch`, `/api/cell/frame/calibrate`, `/api/cell/target`, `/api/cell/target/touchup`, `/api/cell/target/undo`, `/api/cell/target/delete`, `/api/cell/pattern`, `/api/cell/pattern/delete`, `/api/cell/pattern/counter`, `/api/cell/interlock`, `/api/cell/interlock/delete`, `/api/cell/zone` (with `interlocks`), `/api/cell/zone/delete`, `/api/program`, `/api/program/delete`, `/api/run/start`, `/api/run/pause`, `/api/run/resume`, `/api/run/step`, `/api/run/stop`, `/api/run/reset`, `/api/run/override`, `/api/moveto` and `/api/plan` (to a `target`, or a `pattern`'s `slot` or next slot), `/api/home`, `/api/gripper`, `/api/calls/ack`, `/api/io/set`, `/api/freedrive`, `/api/manual` and `/api/hold`, besides the jog panel's own `/api/enable`, `/api/stop`, `/api/jog` and `/api/move`. `GET /api/status` also reports what each gripper holds (`held`), the patterns' counters (`counters`) and the calls not yet seen (`calls`); `GET /api/io` the interlocks and grippers as they read. `scripts/jog_server.py` (`api_get`, `api_post`) documents their bodies, and `scripts/runner.py` the steps.
